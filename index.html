What does research tell us about AI and student learning?

Research into AI tutoring shows promise, but the way it’s used matters. The important question is what students can understand, remember, and do independently afterwards.

01

The learning happens when they take part.

An explanation is a starting point. Trying a step, explaining their reasoning, and tackling another problem give students a chance to turn that explanation into understanding.

02

Getting it right isn’t the same as learning it.

Getting something right with help is a start. Returning to it later and trying again without support gives a clearer picture of what they’ve actually learned.

03

How AI helps makes a difference.

Studies have found positive, mixed, and negative results. The structure of the activities, the feedback students receive, and the support around them all matter.

Peer-Reviewed Randomized Controlled Trial

Students learned more than twice as much.

In a peer-reviewed randomized controlled trial (Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, 2025), university physics students using a purpose-built Socratic AI tutor achieved more than double the learning gains of students in an active-learning classroom — in less time.
